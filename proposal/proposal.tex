\section{Motivation}
In real robotic tasks, the most natural reward specification is binary: the
task was accomplished or it was not. Dense, informative rewards must instead
be hand-engineered, which requires domain knowledge, is error-prone, and can
bias the learned behavior in unintended ways. Yet standard off-policy
actor-critic methods such as Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) are widely believed to
degrade severely when rewards become sparse, because the replay buffer fills
with uniformly uninformative transitions before the first success is ever
observed. We want to \emph{quantify} this degradation in a controlled,
reproducible experiment on a goal-conditioned robotic reaching task, and then
test whether Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) --- which manufactures
successful experience from failed episodes by goal relabeling --- can recover
dense-reward-like sample efficiency from the sparse signal alone.

\section{Related Topics}
This project connects off-policy maximum-entropy RL, goal-conditioned RL, and
reward design. SAC \cite{haarnoja18} is the off-policy actor-critic backbone
we study. HER \cite{andrychowicz17} is the goal-relabeling mechanism under
test; it was originally demonstrated with DDPG on the Fetch robotics suite,
where sparse-reward learning without relabeling failed on all tasks except
reaching. The Fetch environments and the sparse/dense reward variants we will
use are standardized in the multi-goal RL benchmark of Plappert et
al.~\cite{plappert18}, now maintained as Gymnasium-Robotics.

\section{Idea}
We will run a three-way controlled comparison of SAC on
\texttt{FetchReach-v3}, a 7-DoF simulated arm that must move its gripper to a
random 3D goal within 50 steps:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{dense}: vanilla SAC with the informative reward
          $r = -\| x_{\mathrm{grip}} - g \|$;
    \item \textbf{sparse}: vanilla SAC with the binary reward of
          Eq.~\ref{eq:reward};
    \item \textbf{sparse + HER}: identical to (2), but the replay buffer
          additionally stores each episode under relabeled goals
          (Alg.~\ref{alg:her}).
\end{enumerate}

All three conditions share the same SAC hyperparameters, network sizes, seeds
and step budget, so the reward signal (and, in condition 3, the relabeling)
is the only experimental variable. The goal-conditioned sparse reward is

\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:reward}
    r(s, a, g) \;=\; -\,\mathbb{1}\!\left[\;\| x_{\mathrm{grip}}(s) - g
    \| \;>\; \varepsilon\;\right], \qquad \varepsilon = 5\,\mathrm{cm},
\end{equation}

which is $-1$ on every step outside the success region and $0$ inside it ---
a policy that never reaches the goal receives the same return ($-50$)
regardless of how close it got, so the critic receives no gradient toward the
goal until exploration succeeds by chance. Our hypotheses: (i) sparse SAC
will learn substantially slower and less reliably than dense SAC; (ii) HER
will close most of that gap without any reward shaping.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
    \caption{HER goal relabeling (\emph{future} strategy), after each episode.}
    \label{alg:her}
    \begin{algorithmic}
        \Require episode $(s_0, a_0, \dots, s_T)$ with goal $g$, relabels per
                 transition $k$
        \For{$t = 0, \dots, T-1$}
            \State store $(s_t, a_t, r(s_t, a_t, g), s_{t+1}, g)$ in buffer
            \For{$i = 1, \dots, k$}
                \State sample future index $\tau \sim \mathcal{U}(t+1, T)$
                \State $g' \gets$ achieved gripper position in $s_\tau$
                \State store $(s_t, a_t, r(s_t, a_t, g'), s_{t+1}, g')$
                       \Comment{often a success: $r = 0$}
            \EndFor
        \EndFor
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experiments}
\paragraph{Environments \& Metrics}
\texttt{FetchReach-v3} and \texttt{FetchReachDense-v3} from
Gymnasium-Robotics (MuJoCo), with SAC from Stable-Baselines3
(\texttt{MultiInputPolicy} for the dictionary observations). Every 2{,}000
training steps we evaluate the deterministic policy on 20 held-out episodes
and record the \textbf{success rate} (gripper within 5\,cm of the goal at
episode end), the primary metric that is directly comparable across reward
types. From these curves we derive: median \textbf{steps-to-90\%-success},
\textbf{area under the success curve} (sample efficiency), and \textbf{final
success rate}; we additionally log training-time success rates and episode
returns as secondary evidence.

\paragraph{Experimental Scope}
$3$ conditions $\times$ $5$ seeds $\times$ $100$k environment steps $= 15$
runs. We will not perform per-condition hyperparameter optimization ---
doing so would confound the comparison; instead all conditions use one fixed,
literature-derived configuration (rl-baselines3-zoo values for Fetch tasks:
$\gamma = 0.95$, lr $10^{-3}$, batch 256, buffer $10^6$; SB3 defaults
otherwise). Planned analysis includes per-seed learning curves (sparse-reward
learning is expected to be high-variance, which aggregate curves hide). As a
stretch goal, an ablation of the HER relabeling ratio (\texttt{n\_sampled\_goal}
$\in \{1, 4, 8\}$) on a subset of seeds.

\paragraph{Estimated Computational Load}
SAC on FetchReach is small (two-layer MLPs, single environment): we estimate
$\sim$20 minutes per 100k-step run on a single GPU, i.e.\ $\approx 5$
GPU-hours for the full grid, executed as a SLURM job array (1 GPU, 4 CPUs,
16\,GB per task) on the KISSKI cluster. Memory and storage needs are modest
($<$1\,GB replay buffer per run; a few MB of logs per run). A repeat of the
full grid for debugging or the ablation fits comfortably within 15 additional
GPU-hours.
